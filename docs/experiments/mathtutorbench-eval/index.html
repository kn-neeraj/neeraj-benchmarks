EXPERIMENT 01 · model-comparison · Complete

MathTutor-Bench: Kimi K3 vs DeepSeek V4 Flash vs GLM-5.2

Three open-weight models write the next tutor turn in a math dialog; an LLM judge scores each turn against the real human teacher's response.

2026-08-30
01

Task

In this experiment, we are evaluating tutoring skill, or pedagogy. Given a real math problem and a partial student/teacher conversation, the model writes the next teacher turn (max 2 sentences). A good turn nudges the student toward their mistake with a question or hint; a bad one either solves it for them or misses the point entirely. Scale: 322 of 327 dialogs.

02

Methodology

0–1 score from an LLM judge (Qwen3.8-Max) comparing the model's turn to the real human teacher's turn; 0.50 means judged equal to the human.

01

Dataset

MathTutor-Bench (hard scaffolding subset) · License: CC BY-SA 4.0 (treated as the more restrictive of two conflicting license statements in the source repo)

02

Judge prompt

You are grading a math tutoring dialog. A student is working through the
problem below with a teacher. You will see the problem, the conversation so
far, the teacher's ACTUAL human response, and a CANDIDATE response written
by a different teacher for the same turn.

Problem: {question}
Reference solution (for your own context only - do not require the teacher
turn to reveal this): {reference_solution}

Conversation so far:
{dialog_history}

ACTUAL human teacher response: {ground_truth_response}

CANDIDATE teacher response: {candidate_response}

Score the CANDIDATE response from 0.0 to 1.0 on how well it serves as the
next teacher turn, judged AGAINST the actual human response as the
reference point. A score of 0.50 means the candidate is judged equal in
quality to the actual human response. Higher than 0.50 means better than
the human response; lower means worse. Consider: does it give a helpful
nudge, question, or hint (not the full answer)? Is it at most two
sentences? Is it appropriate given the conversation so far? Do not reward
responses that reveal the final answer.

Respond with ONLY a JSON object of the exact form
{"score": <float between 0.0 and 1.0>, "rationale": "<one sentence>"},
with no other text.
03

Notes & limitations

  • Judge is Qwen3.8-Max, deliberately not one of the three models under test, to reduce self-preference and shared-lineage bias.

Behind the numbers

Getting clean reasoning-mode data took real debugging: a token-budget starvation bug and a stop-sequence bug that matched inside models' hidden reasoning traces both had to be found and fixed first — see the experiment README for the full three-attempt story.

Example dialog

Problem: Bob buys 50 feet of rope. He uses a 5th of it to make a small piece of art. He takes the rest and gives half of it to the friend. After that, he cuts 2-foot sections. How many sections does he get?

…previous conversation
Teacher

Nope! After Bob made the artwork he had 40 feet left, then he gave half to the friend, so he had 20 feet left. So how many 2 foot sections could he make from that?

Student

Oh, right! After Bob gave half of the rope to the friend, he would have 20 feet of rope left. Then, if he divided that into 2-foot sections, he would get 10 sections. So, the total number of sections he would have is 10 + 10 = 20.

Teacher (actual human)

So you said if he divided it into 2-foot sections he would have 10 sections. Why are you adding another 10 section to that?

Model responses for this turn
Kimi K3 score: 0.1

You're very close! Remember, the 10 sections are from the 20 feet Bob gave to his friend, not from the 20 feet he kept for himself. So, how many sections can he make from the 20 feet he kept?

DeepSeek V4 Flash score: 0.2

You had the right idea when you first divided 40 by 2 to get 20 sections from the rope he kept for himself. Now, think carefully: after he gives half of the 40 feet to his friend, how much rope does he actually have left to cut into 2-foot sections?

GLM-5.2 score: 0.9

You're right that he gets 10 sections from the 20 feet he kept — but why are you adding the extra 10? Remember, the first 10 feet went into the artwork, so it's not available to be cut into sections.

04

Takeaways

  • GLM-5.2 is the strongest tutor of the three, with a statistically meaningful edge — n=322 is large enough that this gap isn't sampling noise.
  • Reasoning consistently helps pedagogical scaffolding, for all three models.
